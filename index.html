About me

Payments taught me to build things that can't fail quietly.

As a Senior Android Developer at areeba, I develop the Android app that runs on our payment terminals — Nexgo and Kozen devices such as the UN20, N62 and N92. A customer taps a card, and my code reads it, talks to the bank, prints the receipt and tells the cash register or vending machine what happened.

That work means EMV card flows, PIN entry, reversals and currency conversion, plus integrations with cash registers over serial and Wi-Fi and with vending machines over MDB. When the hardware isn't on my desk, I build the tools to test without it.

Before areeba I spent almost seven years at BMB Group, building a B2B merchandising app from scratch with offline sync and receipt printing. I started in 2014 at Code Fish, building HR systems in C# and .NET for clients such as GlobeMed.

AI is part of how I work every day. I run coding agents such as Claude Code with project rules, custom skills and tool connections, and I check every AI-written change with tests and on the real device. In my apps I use on-device machine learning, speech recognition and text-to-speech where they make the app genuinely better.

As a freelancer I take that discipline to mobile apps, Windows apps and websites for businesses: clear requirements, small working releases, and code that's tested before it ships. Out of respect for my role at areeba, I don't build POS or payment apps for anyone else.

Vending machines that take cards

KotlinMDBNexgo UN20 / N62

Turned our payment terminal into the card reader inside vending machines. The machine asks for a price over the MDB bus; the terminal takes the card and tells the machine whether to release the item.

  • Upgraded the MDB library and moved prices to 32-bit, so high-value items charge the right amount.
  • Automatic refund when a paid item doesn't drop, or the machine loses power mid-sale.
  • Self-healing connection and a clear vending screen, tuned for both a phone-size and a square 480×480 terminal.

FAQ

Hiring a freelance developer in Lebanon

Quick answers to what clients usually ask first.

Are you available as a freelance developer in Lebanon?

Yes. I'm based in Baabda, next to Beirut, and I take freelance projects from businesses in Beirut and across Lebanon. Most of the work happens remotely, with regular builds you can try on your own phone or computer.

What kind of apps and websites can you build?

Native Android apps in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, Windows desktop apps in C# and WPF, and websites and web apps. I also connect software to machines such as printers, barcode scanners, and serial or USB devices.

Do you build POS or payment apps?

No. I work full-time as a Senior Android Developer at areeba, a payments company, so I don't take freelance projects for POS, payment terminal, card payment or other payment apps that would compete with areeba. It's a matter of professionalism. I'm happy to help with any other kind of app or website.

Do you use AI to build apps?

Yes. I work with coding agents such as Claude Code every day, which makes delivery faster. I review, test and understand every change before it reaches you, and I can add AI features to your app too: on-device recognition, voice commands, and text-to-speech in Arabic and English.

How do you price a project?

After a short call I send a written scope with screens and milestones, and a fixed price or a day rate — your choice. You know what you're getting and when before any work starts.

Can you fix or update an app that already exists?

Yes. If an app crashes, has drifted from its backend or needs a new SDK version, I find the cause, fix it, and add tests so it stays fixed.

How fast do you reply?

Within one working day, with questions or a first estimate. The quickest way to reach me is WhatsApp or email.

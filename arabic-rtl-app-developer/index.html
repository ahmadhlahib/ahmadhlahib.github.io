Arabic is more than a translation file

Most apps that "support Arabic" just swap the strings. Users then see icons pointing the wrong way, numbers and phone numbers scrambled, text cut off, and English words jumping to the wrong side of a sentence. To an Arabic reader the app looks broken, and they leave.

I build Arabic interfaces in my own projects and at work, and I read them the way your users will. This website has a full Arabic version; you can switch to it and judge the result yourself.

Common Arabic mistakes I fix

  • Layouts built with left/right, so nothing flips in Arabic.
  • Back arrows and progress bars pointing the wrong way.
  • Phone numbers, card-style numbers and prices shown reversed or split.
  • English words or brand names landing at the wrong end of an Arabic sentence.
  • Truncated text because Arabic strings are longer or taller than the English ones.
  • Letter-spacing that disconnects Arabic letters.
  • Dates and digits in the wrong numeral system for the country.

Questions clients ask

What is RTL support in an app?

RTL means right-to-left. Arabic, Hebrew, Persian and Urdu are read from right to left, so the whole interface mirrors: navigation, alignment, icons and the order of elements, while some things such as numbers and media controls stay left-to-right.

How long does it take to add Arabic to an existing app?

It depends on how the layouts were built. An app that already uses start and end alignment can take a few days; one built with fixed left and right positioning takes longer. I start with an audit so you know before committing.

Do you translate the text too?

I review and correct Arabic wording in context as a native speaker. For large volumes of marketing text I work alongside your translator and check the result inside the app.

Do you support other RTL languages?

The layout work is the same for Hebrew, Persian, Urdu and Kurdish. I can make the interface right-to-left for any of them; the wording review I do myself only in Arabic.

Which platforms do you cover?

Android in Kotlin, with Jetpack Compose or XML, iOS through Kotlin Multiplatform, and websites and web apps.

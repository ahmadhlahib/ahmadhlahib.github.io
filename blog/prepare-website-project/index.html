Hiring a web developer in Beirut? What to prepare first

The fastest and cheapest website projects I build are the ones where the client arrives prepared. Here is the checklist I send before any work starts, and why each item matters.

By Ahmad El Lahib · freelance web developer in Baabda, Lebanon

Short answer: before you contact a web developer in Beirut, prepare eight things: a one-sentence goal, the list of pages, your texts (even as notes), your logo, real photos, a domain in your own name, three websites you like, and your budget range with one person who approves the work. With these ready, a company website usually takes one to three weeks instead of two months.

1. One sentence: what should the website do?

Not "we need a website", but what a visitor should do after reading it. For example: "People searching for a dentist in Hamra should book a visit on WhatsApp." That sentence decides the pages, the buttons and what Google should find you for.

  • Who is the visitor? A customer, a supplier, someone applying for a job?
  • What is the one action: call, WhatsApp, fill a form, visit the shop, order?
  • Which areas do you serve: one neighbourhood, Beirut, all of Lebanon, abroad?

2. The list of pages

Write the pages as a simple list. Most company websites in Lebanon need five to eight:

  1. Home: who you are, what you offer, the main button.
  2. Services or products: one page per main service is best for Google.
  3. About: your story, team, years of experience.
  4. Work or gallery: projects, before and after photos, menu.
  5. Prices or packages, if you are comfortable showing them.
  6. FAQ: the questions customers already ask you on the phone.
  7. Contact: phone, WhatsApp, map, opening hours.

Fewer, clear pages are better than many empty ones. You can add pages later.

3. Your texts, even as rough notes

This is the item that delays most projects. You don't need polished writing, but you do need the facts, because only you know them: what you offer, for whom, your prices or price ranges, your areas, your hours and why customers choose you.

  • Bullet points in Arabic or English are fine; the developer or a copywriter turns them into web text.
  • For an Arabic and English site, decide who writes or checks each language. Machine translation alone reads badly to customers.
  • Collect the questions people ask you every week. They become your FAQ, which helps both customers and Google.

4. Logo, colours and photos

  • Logo in the best file you have: SVG or a large PNG with a transparent background, not a WhatsApp screenshot.
  • Colours and fonts if you already use them on your shop, cards or social pages.
  • Real photos of your place, team, products and work. Phone photos are fine in daylight, horizontal, without heavy filters. Real photos build more trust than stock images.
  • Only use photos you own or have permission to use.

5. A domain and accounts in your own name

Buy the domain yourself (about $10–20 a year for a .com) or ask the developer to register it with your name and your login. The same goes for hosting, Google Search Console and your Google Business Profile. If the developer disappears, you must still own your website.

6. Three websites you like

Send three links, from Lebanon or anywhere, and one line for each: "I like how clear the prices are", "I like the photos at the top". This saves a full round of design changes. Also send one site you don't like, and why.

7. What you want Google to find you for

Write the words your customers would type, for example "dentist Hamra", "car rental Beirut" or "web development Beirut". Add the exact business name, address and phone you use everywhere, written the same way each time. That consistency matters for your Google setup and your Business Profile.

8. Budget range, deadline and one decision maker

  • Give a budget range. It lets the developer propose what fits, instead of guessing.
  • Say if there is a real deadline: an opening, an event, a season.
  • Choose one person who approves designs and texts. Feedback from five people in a WhatsApp group is the second biggest cause of delays.

How a prepared project runs

For a company website of about six pages in Arabic and English, a typical timeline when the content is ready:

  1. Day 1: a short call, then a written fixed quote with the page list.
  2. Days 2–4: design of the home page on a private preview link you open on your phone.
  3. Days 5–9: all pages in both languages, contact buttons, map and Google setup.
  4. Days 10–12: one round of your feedback, testing on real phones, launch.

Without texts and photos, the same project can easily take two months.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Choosing the cheapest quote without checking what Google setup is included.
  • Adding new pages after the design is approved, then expecting the same price and date.
  • Waiting for "perfect" photos instead of launching with good ones and updating later.
  • An Arabic version that is only translated text inside a left-to-right layout.

FAQ

What do I need before hiring a web developer?

A one-sentence goal, the list of pages, your logo, real photos, texts or at least notes for each page, a domain name in your own name, three websites you like, your budget range and one person who approves the work.

Should I write the website texts myself?

You know your business best, so write the facts yourself, even as bullet points in Arabic or English. A developer or copywriter can turn them into clear web texts, but nobody can invent your prices, services and experience.

Do I need my own domain before the project starts?

It helps. Buy the domain in your own name with your own login, for about $10 to $20 a year, and share access with the developer. Never let a developer register it in their name.

How do I make my website project cheaper and faster?

Have the content ready before work starts, start with fewer pages, use a WhatsApp button instead of a booking system, and give feedback in one list per round instead of many small messages.

Why do website projects in Lebanon get delayed?

Most delays come from the client side: texts and photos that arrive late, several people giving different feedback, and new pages added halfway through. A clear page list and one decision maker fix most of it.

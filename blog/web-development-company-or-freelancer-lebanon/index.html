Web development company or freelancer in Lebanon? How to choose

Search for web development companies in Lebanon and you get agencies, directories and freelancers side by side. Both can build a good website. This guide explains what you actually pay for with each, and how to check either one before you sign.

By Ahmad El Lahib · freelance web developer in Baabda, Lebanon. I am a freelancer, so I have tried to be fair to agencies too.

Short answer: choose a web development company for big projects: online shops with card payments and stock systems, portals with many user roles, or work that needs invoices, tenders and a team on call. Choose a freelancer for a company website, landing page, catalogue or bilingual site of up to about fifteen pages, where you want to talk directly to the person building it and pay roughly half or a third of an agency price. Either way, keep the domain and accounts in your own name.

What a web development company gives you

An agency in Beirut is a team: usually a project manager, a designer, one or more developers, and sometimes content writers, SEO and social media people. That brings real advantages:

  • Capacity: several people can work in parallel, so large projects move.
  • Continuity: if one developer leaves or gets sick, someone else continues.
  • Specialists: brand design, copywriting, ads and SEO under one contract.
  • Paperwork: registered company, invoices, formal contracts, which some companies and tenders require.

The trade-offs: you pay for that structure (agencies in Lebanon often quote two to three times a freelancer's price), you mostly talk to an account manager rather than the developer, and small changes can wait in a queue behind bigger clients. Some agencies also pass smaller projects to junior staff or outside freelancers.

What a freelance web developer gives you

  • Direct contact: the person you message on WhatsApp is the person writing the code.
  • Lower price: no office, sales team or project manager in the quote.
  • Speed on small projects: a prepared company website can go live in one to three weeks.
  • Flexibility: small edits after launch are often quick and cheap.

The trade-offs: one person has limited capacity, holidays and sick days stop the work, and a freelancer may not cover brand design, photography or paid ads. The real risk is a freelancer who disappears with your domain or files, which you avoid with the checks below.

When a company is the better choice

  • An online shop with card payments, stock and delivery integrations.
  • A portal or web app with logins, dashboards and many user roles.
  • A large organisation where several departments approve the work.
  • You need a registered supplier for invoices, procurement or a tender.
  • You want design, website, ads and social media from one team every month.

When a freelancer is the better choice

  • A company, clinic, restaurant, school or service website of five to fifteen pages.
  • A landing page for a campaign, an opening or one product.
  • A catalogue where customers order on WhatsApp or pay cash on delivery.
  • An Arabic and English website for a small or medium business.
  • You want to speak directly with the developer and keep the budget under control.

7 checks before you sign, with anyone

  1. Open their live work on your phone. Not screenshots: real websites you can open, scroll and click. Check the Arabic pages too.
  2. Ask who actually builds it. The senior person you met, a junior, or an outside freelancer?
  3. Domain, hosting and Google accounts in your name. Search Console and your Google Business Profile included.
  4. A written page list and a fixed price. Plus what counts as an extra and how much it costs.
  5. What Google setup is included. Page titles, speed on phones, Search Console, sitemap, Business Profile, Arabic done properly.
  6. What happens after launch. How long free fixes last, the price of edits, who renews the domain.
  7. Handover. You receive the files and logins at the end, even if you later change developer.

Red flags

  • No live examples, only designs or screenshots.
  • The domain registered in the developer's or agency's name.
  • A monthly fee you must keep paying to "own" your website.
  • A bought template with your logo, sold as a custom design.
  • A promise of "first page on Google in a week". Nobody can promise that.

FAQ

Is a web development company in Lebanon better than a freelancer?

Neither is better in general. A company is the safer choice for large projects with many integrations, many stakeholders or a need for invoices and tenders. A freelancer is usually faster and cheaper for a company website, landing page or catalogue of five to fifteen pages.

How much more does a web development company charge than a freelancer?

In Lebanon in 2026, agencies usually quote two to three times a freelancer's price for the same website. A company website that costs about $400 to $1,500 with a freelancer is often $1,000 to $4,500 with an agency, because the price also covers project managers, designers and office costs.

What is the biggest risk with a freelance web developer?

Depending on one person. Reduce it by keeping the domain, hosting and Google accounts in your own name, getting the source files at the end, and agreeing in writing how support and changes work after launch.

What should I check before signing with any web developer?

Open their live work on your phone, ask who actually builds the site, keep the domain and hosting in your name, get a written page list and fixed price, ask what Google setup is included, and ask what support costs after launch.

Can a freelancer build an Arabic and English website?

Yes, if they have done right-to-left layouts before. Ask to see a live Arabic page on your phone: menus, numbers, phone numbers and forms should all read correctly from right to left, not just translated text in a left-to-right layout.

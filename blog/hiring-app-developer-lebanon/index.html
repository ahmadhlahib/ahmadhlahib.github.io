Hiring an app developer in Lebanon: 10 questions to ask before you pay

Most app projects that go wrong don't fail on code. They fail on things nobody asked about at the start: who owns the app, who can update it, what "done" means. Ask these ten questions before you pay anyone, including me.

By Ahmad El Lahib · freelance developer in Baabda, Lebanon

Ownership and access

  1. "Will the app be published under my own Google Play and Apple accounts?" The answer must be yes. If the app lives in the developer's account, you don't really own it: you can't update it, move it or keep it alive if they disappear.
  2. "Do I get the full source code, and where will it live?" Ask for a Git repository (GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket) in your company's name, with access from day one, not a zip file at the end.
  3. "Who owns the domain, the server and the database?" Domain, hosting, Firebase project and email should all be registered to your business. The developer gets access; you keep ownership.

Scope and money

  1. "Can I have the scope in writing?" A short document with the screens, the users and what is not included. It protects both sides, and a developer who won't write one will argue about scope later.
  2. "How do you split the payments?" Pay against milestones you can see and try, for example a first working build, then the full feature set, then the store release. Avoid paying most of the price up front.
  3. "What happens when I want to change something?" Changes are normal. Agree in advance how they are priced: as a small fixed add-on, or at the day rate.

Quality

  1. "How often will I see a working version?" Every week or two is a good answer. If the first thing you can install is the "final" app after three months, you have no way to steer.
  2. "How do you test, and on which devices?" Look for real phones (including cheaper Android models your customers actually use), automated tests on the important logic, and a short test checklist before each release.
  3. "Will it work properly in Arabic?" Ask to see a right-to-left screen from a previous project. Mirrored layouts, numbers, phone numbers and mixed Arabic/English text are where weak apps break.

After launch

  1. "What happens after the release?" Google and Apple change their rules every year, and an app that isn't updated can be removed from the store. Ask about a warranty period for bugs, and the cost of yearly updates.

Red flags

  • No examples you can install, open or see running.
  • A price given before anyone asked what the app does.
  • "We'll publish it under our account, it's easier."
  • No written scope, or a scope that is one line long.
  • You can't reach the person writing the code.

FAQ

Should I hire a freelancer or an agency?

A freelancer is usually more direct and costs less; an agency makes sense when you need several people at once. Either way, these ten questions apply.

Is it normal to pay a deposit?

A small deposit to book the time is normal. The rest should follow milestones you can see and test.

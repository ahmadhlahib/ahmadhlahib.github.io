Case study: two local business websites built to be found on Google

In November 2026 I built two German websites for car-buying businesses in Bochum. Both had to bring customers from Google, not just look good. Here is exactly how I planned and built them, and what happened in the first week.

By Ahmad El Lahib · freelance web developer in Baabda, Lebanon

Short answer: a website gets found on Google when it has one clear page for every service and every area you serve, real answers to customers' questions, fast loading, correct structured data, and a Google Business Profile with the same name, address and phone everywhere. For these two projects, Google listed the new pages within days and the business name reached page 1 in the first week. The competitive keywords take months, and that is normal.

The two projects

  • Revier Autoankauf buys used cars, accident cars and vans in Bochum and the Ruhr area.
  • Unfallwagen Zentrale buys damaged and scrap vehicles, including buses and trucks, from a yard in Bochum.

Both businesses work in the same city and a similar market, so a second challenge was keeping them clearly separate for Google: different names, texts, design and contact details, and no links between the two sites.

1. Plan the pages around what people search

Before any design, I wrote down the searches a customer would type: the service plus the city, such as "Autoankauf Bochum" or "Autoverwertung Gelsenkirchen". Every important search got its own page:

  • Service pages: used cars, accident cars, engine damage, vans and buses, scrap cars.
  • City pages: one per city in the service area, with the districts, the route from Bochum and local details, not the same text with the city name swapped.
  • Guides: articles that answer real questions, for example how to deregister a car or what a damaged car is still worth.
  • Trust pages: how the sale works, FAQ, about, contact, legal pages.

The result was close to 60 pages per site. Each page answers its question in the first two sentences, which also helps AI assistants like ChatGPT quote it.

2. Build it fast and simple

Instead of WordPress with plugins, I wrote a small generator: one settings file with the business name, address, phone, hours and service area, and one short file per page. It produces plain HTML that is hosted on Cloudflare Pages.

3. Tell Google exactly who the business is

Every page carries structured data that Google reads directly: the business type, address, map coordinates, opening hours, service area, breadcrumbs and the FAQ of that page. I also added an llms.txt summary for AI assistants and a sitemap with every page.

4. Get the pages indexed quickly

  • Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools connected on launch day, with the sitemap submitted.
  • IndexNow pings after every update, so Bing and other search engines hear about new pages within minutes.
  • For the most important new pages, a manual indexing request in Search Console.

5. Everything outside the website

For a local business, Google looks at much more than the site itself. Together with the owners, we set up:

  • a Google Business Profile, verified with a short video of the yard;
  • listings on Bing Places, Apple Business and German directories such as Das Örtliche and Gelbe Seiten;
  • a Facebook page, and printed cards with a QR code that asks real customers for a review.

The name, address and phone are written exactly the same way everywhere. That consistency is one of the strongest local signals.

6. Keep two similar businesses apart

Two car buyers in one city can look like one company with two websites, which Google may treat as duplicate or spam. So each site has its own name, brand, design and specialisation, all texts were written separately, and I measured the text overlap between them: under 10% on every page, mostly the contact lines.

What happened in the first week

  • Google indexed the new pages within days of launch.
  • Searches for the business names showed the sites on page 1.
  • The first visitors came from Google search within the first week.
  • Broad terms such as "Autoankauf Bochum", where established competitors and directories rank, are not on page 1 yet. That usually takes months of reviews, listings and new content.

I keep improving both sites every day: new city pages where Search Console shows impressions, longer pages where they rank on page 2, and new guides for questions people actually search.

What this means for a business in Lebanon

The same method works in Beirut and the rest of Lebanon, in Arabic and English:

  1. One page per service and per area you really serve, for example Hamra, Achrafieh, Jounieh or Saida.
  2. Answers to the questions customers already ask you on WhatsApp.
  3. A fast website you own, on your own domain.
  4. A verified Google Business Profile and the same name, address and phone everywhere.
  5. Real reviews from real customers, asked for after every job.

FAQ

How long does it take for a new website to appear on Google?

With Search Console set up and a sitemap submitted, new pages are usually indexed within days. Ranking on page 1 for competitive searches takes longer, often several months, and depends on reviews, listings and content.

Do I need a separate page for every city or area?

If you serve several areas, yes: one page per area with real local information helps Google match you to local searches. Copying the same text and only changing the city name does not help and can hurt.

Do I need WordPress for good SEO?

No. A fast static website ranks just as well and costs less to run. WordPress makes sense when you want to edit many pages yourself every week.

What matters most for local SEO besides the website?

A verified Google Business Profile, the same business name, address and phone on every listing, and real customer reviews. Together with a clear website, these decide most local rankings.

Does my business need an app or a website?

Many businesses ask for an app when a good website would do the job for a fraction of the cost, and a few do the opposite. Here's how to decide.

By Ahmad El Lahib · freelance developer in Baabda, Lebanon

Start with a website if…

  • People need to find you: on Google, on the map, from an Instagram or Facebook bio.
  • You want them to contact you: call, WhatsApp, book a visit, ask for a quote.
  • Customers visit once in a while, not every day. Nobody installs an app for a business they use twice a year.
  • You need to look credible to companies you sell to.

A fast, mobile-friendly site in Arabic and English, with a clear WhatsApp button and a Google Business Profile, covers most small and medium businesses in Lebanon.

An app pays off when…

  • Customers use you often: ordering, booking, tracking a delivery, loyalty points.
  • You need the phone's hardware: camera, GPS, Bluetooth, barcode scanning, printing, working offline.
  • You want push notifications to bring people back.
  • Your staff need a tool in the field: sales reps, drivers, technicians, merchandisers. Internal apps are often the best return of all, because they save hours every day.

When you need both

If you build an app, you still need a website: the stores require a privacy policy page, and people search for you on Google before they install anything. A common path is to launch the website first, learn what customers ask for, then build the app around the features they use most.

What about a web app?

A web app runs in the browser, like an online dashboard. It's a good middle ground for internal tools used on computers, and for customer features that don't need the phone's hardware. It won't appear on Google Play or the App Store, and it can't do everything a native app can on a phone.

A quick test

Ask yourself: will the same person use this every week? If yes, consider an app. If no, start with an excellent website and invest the difference in being found on Google.
